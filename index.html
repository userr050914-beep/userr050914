Calculating the "Peak of Growth"

Using derivatives to optimize Oil Palm yields in Malaysia. Understanding when a biological asset reaches its maximum rate of production.

🌴 The Bioscience Problem

In Malaysia, Oil Palm (Elaeis guineensis) typically follows a sigmoidal (S-shaped) growth curve. For Food Security managers, the goal isn't just total yield, but knowing the Rate of Change.

"The derivative \(f'(x)\) represents the Marginal Productivity. When \(f'(x)\) starts to decline, it indicates the tree is entering its senescence phase, critical for replanting schedules."

The Mathematics

Yield Function \(f(x)\)

\[ f(x) = \frac{25}{1 + e^{-0.4(x - 8)}} \]

Estimates tonnes/hectare over year \(x\).

Derivative \(f'(x)\)

\[ f'(x) = \frac{10e^{-0.4(x-8)}}{(1 + e^{-0.4(x-8)})^2} \]

Calculates the rate of yield increase per year.

Data Context: Based on Malaysian Palm Oil Board (MPOB) standard yield curves for DxP planting materials.
